# Ratios and mixtures

## Formulas
- Divide T in a:b → aT/(a+b) and bT/(a+b)
- Combine a:b and b:c by making the common term equal
- Mixture: when adding one component, the other component's quantity stays fixed

## Solved examples (verified)
1. **Divide 540 in 4:5:** 540/9 = 60 per part, so **240 and 300**.
2. **A:B = 2:3, B:C = 4:5:** make B equal (12): A:B = 8:12, B:C = 12:15, so **8:12:15**.
3. **40 L of milk and water in 3:1; how much water to make it 1:1?** Milk 30, water 10. Milk stays 30, so water must be 30: add **20 L**.

## Traps
- Adding the same amount to both terms does not keep the ratio.
- When combining ratios, scale both to the common term; do not just join them.
- In a mixture, keep the unchanged component fixed.

## Good wrong options
Invert the ratio; use the total as one part; join ratios without scaling.
