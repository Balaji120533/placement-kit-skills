# Simple and compound interest

## Formulas
- Simple interest: SI = P × R × T / 100
- Amount with compound interest: A = P(1 + R/100)^T; CI = A − P
- Half-yearly compounding: halve the rate, double the periods
- Difference between CI and SI for 2 years: P(R/100)²
- Doubling by simple interest: years = 100 / R

## Solved examples (verified)
1. **SI on 5000 at 8% for 3 years:** 5000 × 8 × 3 / 100 = **1200**.
2. **CI on 10000 at 10% for 2 years:** 10000 × 1.21 − 10000 = **2100**.
3. **CI minus SI on 5000 at 10% for 2 years:** 5000 × (0.1)² = **50**.
4. **Amount on 8000 at 10% per year, compounded half-yearly, for 1 year:** 8000 × 1.05² = **8820**.
5. **Years to double at 8% simple interest:** 100/8 = **12.5**.

## Traps
- "Amount" and "interest" are different things.
- With half-yearly compounding, change both the rate and the periods.
- The CI − SI shortcut works only for exactly 2 years.

## Good wrong options
The amount when interest is asked; 8800 from simple interest in example 4; forgetting to double the periods.
