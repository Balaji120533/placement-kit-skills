# Verbal: reading comprehension, error spotting, sentence completion

Code can't check these answers. Before showing a question, state to yourself why the correct option is right and why each other option is wrong. Discard the question if two options are defensible.

## Reading comprehension

**Method:** read the question first, then the passage. Answer only from the passage, not from what you know.

**Question types:** main idea, stated detail, inference, tone, meaning in context.

**Example passage (original):** "Small towns often lose their young people to cities. Yet remote work has begun to reverse this for some families, who keep city salaries while paying town rents. Whether this lasts depends on internet quality more than on any government scheme."

- **Main idea?** Remote work may slow the move away from small towns, depending on internet quality.
- **Inference:** the author sees internet quality as more decisive than government schemes. The passage does not say schemes are useless, so an option claiming that is too extreme.

**Traps:** options that are true in general but not in the passage; extreme words (always, never, only); one detail offered as the main idea.

**When generating:** write an original passage of 60 to 120 words. Never reproduce published text.

## Error spotting

**Method:** check subject-verb agreement, then tense, then pronouns, then prepositions.

**Examples:**
- "Neither of the boys **have** finished." → **has**: "neither" is singular.
- "Each of the students **are** ready." → **is**: "each" is singular.
- "He is senior **than** me." → **to**: "senior" takes "to".

**Traps:** a noun between the subject and the verb; "one of the" with a plural noun but a singular verb; "no error" as a real option.

## Sentence completion

**Method:** find the signal word: "although", "but" and "yet" reverse the meaning; "because" and "so" continue it. Predict a word before reading the options.

**Example:** "Although the plan looked ______ on paper, it failed in practice."
Options: promising, doomed, useless, faulty. Answer: **promising**. "Although" requires a contrast with "failed", and the other three all agree with failure.

**Traps:** a word that fits the grammar but not the logic; two blanks where only one word fits.

**When generating:** check grammar and meaning separately, and confirm only one option passes both.
